The Complete Guide to AI Voice Directing, Prosody & Audio Mastering

How to write scripts for neural text-to-speech, select the ideal Kokoro-82M vocal timbre, control breathing and cadence with punctuation, and master 24kHz WAV exports for YouTube, podcasts, and audiobooks.

1. Understanding Neural Prosody in Kokoro-82M

Unlike legacy concatenative or basic parametric text-to-speech systems that read each word in isolation, modern neural acoustic models like Kokoro-82M evaluate entire clauses at once. Before generating a single millisecond of audio, the model converts your raw English text into International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) phoneme sequences paired with stress markers, pitch contours, and prosodic boundary tokens.

Because the neural network predicts pitch rise, vocal warmth, and breath pauses directly from the grammatical structure of a sentence, how you punctuate and structure your script acts as your "director's prompt." Small adjustments to sentence length, punctuation marks, and numeric spelling can transform a flat reading into an expressive, human-grade narration.

How Punctuation Controls Breathing and Inflection

  • Periods (.) — Full Downward Cadence (~350ms–450ms pause): Signals the end of a complete thought. The voice naturally drops in pitch at the final syllable and takes a subtle breath before starting the next sentence.
  • Commas (,) — Mid-Clause Continuation Pause (~150ms–200ms pause): Keeps the vocal pitch slightly elevated on the word preceding the comma, signaling to the listener that the thought is continuing. Avoid placing commas between a subject and verb, as this introduces an unnatural hesitation.
  • Em-Dashes (—) & Semicolons (;) — Dramatic Beat (~250ms–300ms pause): When you want a conversational aside or a dramatic rhetorical pause without dropping pitch all the way to a period, use an em-dash or semicolon.
  • Question Marks (?) — Interrogative Pitch Rise: Automatically shifts the fundamental frequency (F0 contour) upward on the final stressed syllable of the clause, creating genuine curiosity or questioning tone.
  • Exclamation Marks (!) — Elevated Energy & Projection: Increases vocal intensity and slightly raises baseline pitch across the entire sentence. Use sparingly for genuine moments of excitement or urgency.

"In 2026 AI voice generation runs in the browser at 24kHz and costs $0/mo for creators."

3. Formatting Numbers, Acronyms, Technical Terms & Dialogue

Although PureTTS includes an automated Grapheme-to-Phoneme (G2P) normalizer, ambiguous abbreviations or dense technical notation can occasionally be interpreted in multiple ways. Applying these script-prep rules guarantees flawless pronunciation on the first take:

A. Acronyms vs. Initialisms

If an uppercase abbreviation should be spoken letter-by-letter (an initialism), separate the letters with spaces or hyphens if the default dictionary tries to read it as a single word:

  • Write A I or A.I. for clear letter separation, whereas acronyms pronounced as words like NASA or RAM can be left as normal words.
  • For file extensions and protocols, write dot W A V, P D F, H T T P S, or S Q L ("sequel" vs "S Q L") depending on your preferred pronunciation style.

B. Years, Currencies, and Units of Measure

  • Years & Version Numbers: Write v1.0 as version one point oh, and 24kHz as twenty-four kilohertz so the synthesizer applies natural word stress.
  • Equations & Code Snippets: When converting computer science papers or developer documentation, replace raw symbols like O(n log n) with big O of n log n.

C. Multi-Paragraph Chunking for Long-Form Audiobooks

PureTTS automatically splits long manuscripts at sentence boundaries (., !, ?) so that every chunk fits within the optimal neural context window (~250 to 400 characters per pass) before stitching the output into a single seamless 24kHz WAV file. Keeping individual sentences under 45 words ensures maximum vocal expressiveness and prevents run-on breath fatigue.

4. Post-Processing & Mastering 24kHz WAV Exports

One of PureTTS's biggest technical advantages over cloud freemium tools is that it exports uncompressed 24,000 Hz, 16-bit PCM WAV files rather than heavily compressed 128 kbps MP3s. Uncompressed WAV preserves crisp high-frequency consonants and eliminates MP3 ringing artifacts when you import the track into your video editor or Digital Audio Workstation (DAW).

Recommended Mastering Chain (Audacity, DaVinci Resolve Fairlight, or Premiere Pro)

  1. High-Pass Filter (80 Hz, 12 dB/octave): Gently rolls off sub-bass rumble below the fundamental human vocal range, leaving extra headroom in your mix.
  2. Subtle Presence Boost (+1.5 dB wide bell at 3.5 kHz): Helps the voice cut cleanly through background music beds or ambient soundscapes in YouTube videos.
  3. Gentle Optical Compression (2:1 ratio, -18 dB threshold, fast attack, slow release): Evens out dynamic peaks between quiet reflective sentences and emphasized statements by 2 to 3 dB.
  4. Loudness Normalization:
    • YouTube, TikTok & Streaming Video: Normalize integrated loudness to -14 LUFS (with True Peak at -1.0 dBTP).
    • Podcasts (Apple Podcasts / Spotify): Target -16 LUFS stereo or -19 LUFS mono.
    • Audiobooks (ACX / Audible Standard): Maintain RMS levels between -23 dB and -18 dB with peak values no higher than -3.0 dB.
